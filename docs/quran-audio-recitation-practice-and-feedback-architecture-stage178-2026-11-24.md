# Stage 178 — Quran audio recitation practice and feedback architecture

**Date:** 2026-11-24  
**Status:** Product, pedagogy, privacy, and technical design proposal  
**Scope:** Audio listening, learner-controlled recitation practice, optional submissions, and teacher feedback.

## 1. Goals and boundaries

Design a trustworthy audio learning experience that supports Quran reading, fluency, tajwid learning, memorization, and revision. Audio features are learning aids, not proof of mastery. Automated speech or tajwid analysis, if ever considered, must be explicitly labeled as assistive and uncertain; it must not issue authoritative religious judgments or replace a qualified teacher.

This stage does not select a vendor, collect recordings, implement recording, or change application behavior.

## 2. Audio content provenance and integrity

Every recitation asset should carry structured metadata:
- Reciter identity and approved display name.
- Riwaya and relevant recitation tradition metadata.
- Surah and ayah coverage, including exact alignment boundaries where verified.
- Source institution/provider, canonical asset identifier, edition/version, and publication date where known.
- License, permitted uses, attribution text, territory, expiration, and offline/cache permissions.
- Editorial verification status and reviewer record.

Do not publish an asset with missing or unclear usage rights. Never splice, transform, or relabel a recitation in a way that misrepresents the reciter, riwaya, or source. Quran text remains a separate canonical content record using the approved Uthmani edition; audio timestamps must not become the source of truth for ayah text.

## 3. Learner listening experience

Proposed controls:
- Play, pause, seek, and playback speed where the license and product design permit.
- Repeat current ayah, repeat a selected passage, and stop after a configured number of repetitions.
- Move to previous/next verified ayah boundary.
- Display current surah/ayah reference and reciter/riwaya metadata.
- Optional text-follow mode that highlights only verified audio alignment.
- Clear loading, unavailable, offline, and playback-error states.

Avoid automatically starting audio, unexpected volume changes, or hidden autoplay. Provide accessible labels and keyboard/screen-reader operation. Playback speed changes must not be represented as a different riwaya or recitation style.

## 4. Memorization and revision practice modes

Offer distinct, user-controlled modes:
1. **Listen and follow:** learner reads along with a verified text/audio pair.
2. **Listen then recall:** learner listens, pauses, and attempts recall without the text.
3. **Ayah repetition:** learner repeats a bounded ayah or passage.
4. **Revision session:** learner chooses assigned or personally selected review material.
5. **Teacher-assigned practice:** a task references exact surah/ayah range and permitted recitation asset.

A completed playback session is not equivalent to a completed memorization assessment. The learner may mark practice as attempted; mastery and formal assessment remain separate states.

## 5. Optional learner audio submission

Recording must be optional, purpose-specific, and disabled until a clear consent and safeguarding policy is approved.

Before recording:
- Explain what is recorded, why it is requested, who can access it, how long it is retained, and how to withdraw or request deletion.
- Obtain age-appropriate consent and guardian authorization where required by policy and applicable law.
- Offer a non-recording alternative, such as live teacher assessment or self-guided practice.
- Show a clear recording indicator and explicit start/stop/submit controls.
- Do not record in the background or capture unrelated device audio.

Submission metadata should be minimal: learner/resource reference, assigned passage, submission timestamp generated by the server, consent-policy version, and processing state. Avoid embedding names or contact details in filenames or media metadata.

## 6. Teacher review workflow

Teacher review queue should include only learners in classes currently assigned to that teacher and only submissions within the permitted teaching scope.

A review may include:
- Passage and submission reference.
- Teacher-selected rubric dimensions such as fluency, articulation, tajwid application, and memorization accuracy.
- Constructive feedback and a recommended next practice step.
- Review status and server-recorded reviewer/time.
- Optional request for another attempt.

Rubric labels must be defined in age-appropriate language and avoid humiliating comparisons. Distinguish teacher assessment from learner self-report and any automated signal. Corrections to a review should preserve an audit history rather than silently overwrite the original assessment.

## 7. Automated audio analysis — strict limits

No automated scoring is assumed in this design. If a future proposal includes speech recognition, alignment, or tajwid assistance:
- Obtain separate product, privacy, safeguarding, and scholarly approval before implementation.
- Explain supported languages, recitation varieties, limitations, confidence, and known failure modes.
- Do not infer religious commitment, intelligence, disability, or personal traits from voice.
- Do not claim exact tajwid correctness or issue fatwas based on a model output.
- Route uncertain or consequential feedback to a qualified teacher.
- Evaluate across devices, accents, age groups, noise conditions, and supported riwayat using appropriately licensed and consented test data.
- Keep automated output visibly separate from Quran text and teacher judgment.
- Do not use learner recordings to train models unless a separate, explicit, informed permission process is approved.

## 8. Storage, access, and retention

Recordings are sensitive personal data, especially for minors. The default proposal is private storage, short retention, and no public sharing.

Required controls before implementation:
- Private bucket and deny-by-default access.
- Server-side authorization for upload, playback, review, download, and deletion.
- Short-lived signed URLs only after authorization.
- Strict file type, size, duration, and content validation; malware scanning where applicable.
- Random opaque object keys; no names, phone numbers, or other personal data in paths.
- Encryption in transit and documented at-rest protection.
- Retention schedule by submission purpose, with automatic expiry and auditable deletion.
- Access logs that avoid recording audio contents or unnecessary personal metadata.
- No public links, search indexing, or third-party embedding.
- Explicit access revocation after class reassignment, teacher role demotion, guardian-scope change, or account closure.

## 9. Guardian and child-safeguarding boundaries

Guardian access, if approved, must be limited to verified relationships and explicit purposes. A guardian should not automatically receive unrestricted access to every classroom recording or private teacher note. Teacher access is limited to assigned learners and approved educational purpose. No learner-to-learner audio exchange or public audio profile is included in the initial design.

Provide reporting/escalation processes for inappropriate feedback or misuse. Avoid public rankings, popularity metrics, and sharing prompts that pressure children to publish their voices.

## 10. Low-bandwidth and device behavior

- Allow listening at supported quality levels, with clear indication when quality changes.
- Resume downloads safely and verify integrity before playback.
- Do not cache private recordings in shared browser caches.
- Offline recitation downloads require explicit license permission and a documented cache-expiry policy.
- Show download, pending, failed, and available-offline states accurately.
- On logout or account/institution switch, clear private media caches and revoke queued upload actions that are no longer authorized.
- Prevent duplicate submissions with idempotency keys and server-side state transitions.

## 11. Data model concepts (not migrations)

Potential entities for future schema design:
- recitation_assets: approved reciter, riwaya, source, license, version, and editorial status.
- recitation_segments: verified ayah range and timing alignment for an asset version.
- practice_sessions: learner, selected practice mode, passage, and non-sensitive completion metadata.
- audio_submissions: owner, assignment, private object reference, consent version, status, and retention deadline.
- recitation_reviews: reviewer, rubric version, assessment, feedback, and review timestamps.
- audio_access_audit: minimal event records for sensitive access and administrative actions.

These are conceptual names only. Final schema, tenant ownership, composite constraints, RLS, storage policies, retention jobs, and audit semantics require separate review.

## 12. Authorization and lifecycle tests

Use synthetic accounts and synthetic recordings in isolated staging:
- Learner can submit only for self and only when an eligible assignment/consent exists.
- Learner cannot list or play another learner's recording.
- Teacher can access only currently assigned learners and authorized submissions.
- Former teacher loses access after role or assignment revocation.
- Guardian sees only specifically authorized learner summaries, not unrestricted raw audio.
- Institution A cannot access Institution B assets, submissions, or signed URLs.
- Expired/deleted media cannot be fetched through stale links.
- Duplicate, oversized, unsupported, corrupted, or unauthorized uploads are rejected.
- Retention expiry and deletion are verifiable.
- Consent withdrawal blocks new processing and follows the approved deletion/retention policy.
- Offline cache is isolated across accounts and purged on logout.
- Automated feedback, if separately approved, is never presented as definitive teacher judgment.

## 13. Decisions required before implementation

- Which reciters, riwayat, editions, and providers are approved?
- What licenses permit streaming, download, repeat playback, and offline caching?
- Is learner recording necessary, or can the first release use teacher-led live review?
- What age/guardian consent rules and retention periods apply?
- Which roles may listen, review, download, or delete a submission?
- Are any automated audio features in scope at all?
- What rubric and teacher training are required for consistent feedback?

## 14. Explicit non-actions

No audio player, microphone permission, recording interface, speech recognition, tajwid model, audio asset, media upload, storage bucket, signed URL, retention job, database schema, RLS policy, SQL function, migration, consent flow, notification, analytics, Supabase configuration, deployment, merge, or production setting was created or changed. No learner recording or personal audio was accessed. All behaviors are proposals pending licensing, safeguarding, privacy, scholarly, technical, and product approval.
